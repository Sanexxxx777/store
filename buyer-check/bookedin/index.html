Bookedin: what a buyer should know before wiring $1,000,000

bookedin.ai has three years of Stripe history and 100 active subscriptions, so the business is real. But revenue is falling right now, the asking price assumes the opposite, and a visible share of the offer is hands-on service, not software. Do not negotiate from $1M. Negotiate from the churn curve.

Findings

F-01 Revenue is falling: last 30 days are 44 percent of MRR high

What. Stripe-verified revenue for the last 30 days is $5,757. Stated MRR is $13,080. A healthy subscription business shows the two within 10 to 20 percent of each other. A 56 percent gap means one of two things: customers are cancelling faster than the MRR figure has caught up, or MRR is propped up by annual subscriptions that were paid months ago and count as recurring today. Reproduce. Open the public TrustMRR card for bookedin. Compare "Last 30 days" revenue with MRR. Both come from the connected Stripe key, synced 2026-09-04 20:46 UTC. Evidence. TrustMRR markdown card: revenue last 30 days $5,757, MRR $13,080, all-time $305,570, 100 active subscriptions. Ratio 0.44. Meaning for the buyer. The asking price is built on $13k MRR. The cash actually arriving is $5.8k a month. At the current run rate, the multiple you are really paying is closer to 14x annual cash, which is what the seller's own card shows (14.5x). Ask for month-by-month Stripe before any number is discussed.

F-02 "Done-for-you" and consulting are part of the offer high

What. The public site advertises done-for-you setup and consulting alongside the product. Revenue from those lines is labor sold by the current owner, not software. It does not transfer with a repository. Reproduce. Read the homepage copy on bookedin.ai; search for "done-for-you" and "consulting". Evidence. Text of the homepage and pricing section, captured September 5, 2026: both markers present. Meaning for the buyer. You need the split: how much of the $13k MRR (and of the $305k lifetime) came from software subscriptions versus setup, onboarding or consulting invoices. If a third of revenue is service, the software business is worth a third less, and the seller's time is the actual product.

F-03 Domain registered 18 months ago, expires in 161 days medium

What. bookedin.ai was registered on 2025-02-14 and expires 2027-02-14. The product itself reports 38 months of revenue history. The current domain is younger than the business, which usually means a rebrand or a domain change. Reproduce. RDAP lookup for bookedin.ai (rdap.org). Evidence. RDAP record: registered 2025-02-14, expiration 2027-02-14, 161 days left at the time of the check. Meaning for the buyer. Two questions: what was the previous domain and does it still redirect (SEO equity, customer bookmarks, transactional email), and who controls the registrar account. Renew the domain for several years as a closing condition.

F-04 No DMARC record, four security headers missing medium

What. The domain publishes SPF but no _dmarc TXT record. The homepage response has no Content-Security-Policy, X-Frame-Options, X-Content-Type-Options or Referrer-Policy. Reproduce. dig TXT _dmarc.bookedin.ai @1.1.1.1 returns nothing. curl -sI https://bookedin.ai/ shows none of the four headers. Evidence. DNS and header captures, September 5, 2026. Meaning for the buyer. Without DMARC, transactional emails (booking confirmations, receipts) are more likely to land in spam, and the domain can be spoofed in phishing to the product's own customers. The headers are a one-hour fix. Together they say the public surface has never had a security pass.

F-05 No standalone pricing or contact page; tracking scripts fail low

What. /pricing and /plans return 404; /contact and /support return 404. Prices exist only inside the homepage. The console shows three failed requests (HTTP 403) to a third-party funnel tracker. Reproduce. Open the four URLs. Load the homepage with the browser console open. Evidence. HTTP status captures and console log, September 5, 2026. Meaning for the buyer. Cosmetic on its own. Combined with F-04 it points at a marketing site that is maintained lightly. Budget for a cleanup after transfer.

Red flags

  • Revenue falling 56 percent against stated MRR (F-01). Not visible on the listing card at a glance, visible in the Stripe numbers.
  • Service revenue mixed with software revenue (F-02). Transferability unknown.
  • Asking price 3.5x the market median on annual revenue, 14.5x on current cash (verdict, F-01).
  • Domain younger than the business and expiring in 161 days (F-03).
  • No DMARC, no security headers (F-04).

Ask the seller

  1. Read-only Stripe access on a call: MRR by month for 36 months, cancellations by month for the last 6, share of annual versus monthly plans. closed by: live Stripe dashboard.
  2. Revenue split for the last 12 months: software subscriptions versus setup, consulting and any one-time services. closed by: Stripe product-level export plus invoices for services.
  3. Why the last 30 days are $5.8k against $13k MRR: cancellations, annual renewals timing, or a pricing change. closed by: the Stripe export from question 1, not a verbal answer.
  4. Previous domain and whether it redirects; who holds the registrar account; will you renew for 3 years before closing. closed by: registrar screenshot and a redirect test.
  5. Top 5 customers as a share of MRR and whether any of them bought consulting. closed by: Stripe customer export.
  6. Code and process: git log --shortstat, authors, commits in the last 90 days, tests, CI, deployment steps. closed by: screen share of the repository.
  7. Twelve months of invoices for hosting, third-party services and any AI or scheduling APIs the product depends on. closed by: the invoices.
  8. Who does the done-for-you work today and will they stay for a transition period. closed by: named person and a written support commitment.
  9. What is included: domain, GitHub, Stripe, email sending domain, tracking accounts, trademarks. closed by: asset list in the term sheet.
  10. Reason for selling now. closed by: compare with the cancellation curve from question 1.

Method

This review is limited to what is observable from outside the product: the public marketing site, DNS and TLS records, HTTP response headers, and the listing card published on TrustMRR. No code, admin panel, database or paid API was accessed at any point, and no account was created or signed into.

Every network check in this report is a GET request; nothing was submitted, purchased or configured. Revenue figures come from the provider connection TrustMRR displays on the listing (Stripe (API key)), not from a separate audit of the seller's account. This scan ran on September 5, 2026.
