privacy

Your text stays on your Mac

  • Every word is analysed on the spot. No analytics, no keystroke logging.
  • It goes online only with your permission: once a day it checks for updates and sends nothing about you.
  • The verbose log is off. A report you send strips the key codes by itself.
install

Three steps to the first fix

  1. Drag it to Applications

    Open the DMG and move Qwerty Switcher into the Applications folder.

  2. Open it the first time

    The build is signed with a self-signed certificate, so macOS will warn you. On macOS 15 and later: System Settings, Privacy & Security, “Open Anyway”. On 13 and 14: right-click the app, Open.

  3. Allow keyboard access

    The app opens the right settings pane itself. Without this permission it cannot see key presses. No restart needed.

faq

Short answers

Does it work in Telegram, Slack and VS Code?

Yes. The fix is typed one character at a time, so it reaches Electron apps too, where batched keystrokes get lost. In terminals, Latin commands are left alone.

Which languages does it understand?

Russian and English layouts: a 697,000-word dictionary, word frequencies and letter pairs for both languages.

How do updates arrive?

Turn on update checks and auto-install in settings. Once a day the app fetches one file from this site, and permissions survive the update. If you install a new version from the DMG over the old one, macOS will ask for permissions again.

Why is it free, and where is the source?

No subscription, keys or trial. The source code is open on GitHub under the MIT licence.

What if it fixes the wrong thing?

Press ⌘⌥Z and the word goes back to how it was. A word you correct by hand twice gets remembered.
